## Key takeaway
Simple guidance at the right moment builds the confidence learners need to keep going.

---

## The problem
Beginner learners (Mondly's biggest audience) were frustrated and giving up early. The app expected them to know words it had never actually taught them. They didn't feel challenged. They felt lost.

> "You go straight to sentences and words with pictures. How was I supposed to know that beforehand?"
> "The first lessons are already too demanding. Too bad."
> "It doesn't teach you the language. It just shows it to you."

---

## The previous experience
Mondly's approach was simple: start quizzing, learn through repetition. There was also a speaking practice feature, but it lived outside the lesson, felt disconnected, and most users never used it. For beginners, the result was confusion. They felt like they were expected to guess, not learn.

*Lessons opened directly with quizzes. Speaking practice existed but was separate, optional, and easy to miss.*

---

## Research & analysis
I started by looking at how other major players in the language learning space approached vocabulary introduction. In parallel, I ran user interviews to verify the anecdotal evidence we already had and better understand our users' mental models and expectations.

I then partnered with the Data team to dig into behaviour on the app. The finding was telling: learners who were already using the Handsfree feature were retaining better and completing more lessons than those who weren't.

The picture was clear: beginners needed to be taught before being tested. And we already had something worth building on.

The ideal solution? An AI tutor, progressive vocabulary, spaced repetition, pronunciation feedback. But that was months of work, and in the meantime learners would still be dropping off every day.

> **So I reframed the question: what can we do right now, with what we already have, that delivers real value without a massive build?**

---

## The solution
The problem was known. What wasn't obvious was how to fix it without a big commitment. I saw a different path.

After benchmarking competitors and exploring concepts with the Methodology team, I defined the approach around two clear decisions:

**1. Repurpose, don't rebuild.**
We already had a speaking practice feature. Instead of building from scratch, I evolved it into a guided teaching step, integrated directly into the lesson flow. I added ElevenLabs for a natural, human-sounding voice. I kept it optional, so learners who preferred jumping straight to quizzes still could.

**2. Keep validation simple.**
Native speech recognition performed inconsistently across Android and iOS, meaning learners were sometimes marked wrong even when they pronounced correctly, leading to additional frustration. So I simplified: record your voice, listen back, compare. It was enough to deliver the core teaching value, and easy to test cleanly via A/B.

**The flow:**
Mode selection → Listen & repeat → Quiz practice

---

## Impact & results
The experiment worked, and was rolled out at scale.

- +17% lesson completion
- +23.8% conversion to premium within 7 days
- +12% retention

And the clearest signal of all: 76% of users chose the guided practice path over jumping straight to quizzes. The need was real.
